# Milestone 08 · Possession-accounting matchup features

Status: implemented and locally software-tested; real-data results not yet measured. This file specifies the experiment before the user executes it.

## 1. Scope and hypothesis

Tournament outcomes are generated by points scored and allowed. Shooting accuracy is not enough to describe the opportunities to score: turnovers end chances, offensive rebounds create additional attempts, and free throws both produce points and consume some possessions.

**Hypothesis:** a nonlinear, matchup-specific accounting of those quantities adds useful information beyond seeding, adjusted efficiency, basic shooting controls, and direct turnover/rebound/free-throw rates.

This is not a claim that the algebra is a correct game simulator or the most important missing signal. The parameters below are fixed research assumptions, not results from the cited literature. No values are chosen using 2026 tournament outcomes.

## 2. Literature-derived motivation versus new assumptions

Ken Pomeroy’s Four Factors article defines effective field-goal accuracy, turnovers per possession, offensive-rebound share, and FTA/FGA as components of efficiency [1]. His possession estimator is FGA − OR + TO + 0.475 FTA [2]. His rebounding discussion distinguishes rebound opportunity share from total rebound counts, which are affected by missed-shot opportunities [3]. The separate discussion of offensive versus defensive rebounding control provides another reason not to identify a rebound outcome with one team’s skill alone [4].

Those primary sources motivate rates and the possession identity. They **do not validate** our odds-combination rule, 100-opportunity prior, neutral-game approximation, feature contrasts, or classifier results. Those are the experimental choices below. Findings from the cited men’s-college analysis must not simply be assumed to transfer to women’s basketball; both populations are evaluated separately.

Existing repository code already has Four Factors and cross-team products. Six basic rate controls are therefore deliberately not advertised as six newly discovered signals. The reviewed `advanced_features.py` uses products such as offense_A × allowance_B − offense_B × allowance_A. This study instead tests explicit nonlinear possession-accounting contrasts. Novelty is tested against a reference already containing the direct rates.

## 3. Information availability

For each gender and year, only regular-season detailed games through DayNum 132 enter rate aggregation. Shooting controls and strength/efficiency values are reused from verified pre-tournament snapshots. Seeds in the unchanged reference are available only after field announcement; this is not a before-Selection-Sunday forecast.

No tournament label is passed to a rate-building function or matchup-construction function. The workflow reads historical main-draw labels separately to replay known reference predictions and fit/evaluate classifiers. Tournament rows outside 2013–2019 are not used. No 2026 submission or fitted model is produced.

Contemporaneous league priors use that gender-year’s legal regular-season games, not tournament outcomes. This is permissible pre-tournament information, not a claim to pre-game ratings throughout that regular season. Opponent profiles are matchup-conditioned but **not independently schedule-adjusted**. The original adjusted-efficiency reference remains present.

## 4. Raw rate representation and smoothing

For each offensive and allowed side:

- Two-point, three-point, and free-throw makes over attempts; three-point attempt share.
- Turnovers / own estimated possessions, where own possessions = FGA − OR + TO + 0.475 FTA. The individual-side estimator, rather than the averaged two-side estimator, is used to maintain the subsequent algebra.
- Offensive rebounds / (offensive rebounds + opponent defensive rebounds).
- Free-throw attempts / field-goal attempts.

For bounded rates, the fixed prior center is (league successes + 0.5)/(league opportunities + 1). The team estimate is (successes + 100 × center)/(opportunities + 100). For FTA/FGA, the same algebra is a positive rate-shrinkage rule, **not a beta-binomial probability model**; that ratio may exceed one. Empty shooting opportunities inherit the finite prior. Tournament teams require at least ten detailed games. The upstream data snapshot must be byte-identical to the completed experiments.

## 5. Matchup construction

For two-point accuracy, three-point accuracy, three-point share, turnover rate and offensive-rebound share:

`q(A against B) = sigmoid(logit(A offense) + logit(B allowance) − logit(league))`.

Each probability input is clipped to [0.000001, 0.999999] for numerical evaluation. This is a fixed odds-based hypothesis, not learned offense/defense influence weights.

FTA/FGA uses `A offense × B allowance / league`. Free-throw accuracy uses A’s offensive estimate only; opponent free-throw accuracy is not treated as a defender’s free-throw-suppression skill.

Let q2 and q3 be the combined shooting accuracies, s the combined three-point share, a the offensive free-throw accuracy, t the combined turnover rate, r the combined rebound share, and f the combined FTA/FGA. Then:

`g = (1−s)q2 + s q3`

`e = (1−s)q2 + 1.5 s q3`

`PPP = (1−t)(2e + a f) / [1 − r(1−g) + 0.475 f]`.

Derivation: start with P = FGA − OR + TO + 0.475 FTA; substitute TO = tP, FTA = fFGA and OR approximately equal to r(1−g)FGA, then solve for FGA/P and multiply by estimated points/FGA.

The OR substitution neglects team/dead-ball rebounds and rebounds after free throws. It also treats subsequent possessions as having unchanged profiles. These are material approximations. The values are scoring proxies, not guaranteed forecasts of actual efficiency. The denominator is checked positive and all outputs must be finite.

Four matchup candidates (A minus B; per-100 units) are:

1. Complete scoring proxy.
2. Gain from enabling the modeled offensive-rebound rate instead of zero rebounds.
3. Negative cost from enabling turnover rate instead of zero turnovers. A higher difference means A loses less modeled scoring value to turnovers than B.
4. Change from enabling FTA/FGA instead of zero free throws, including the possession denominator effect. This need not always be positive.

The last three are **arithmetic counterfactual contrasts**, not causal estimates and not an additive attribution of total scoring. Interactions mean they need not sum to the complete proxy. All four negate under team swapping.

## 6. Frozen experimental design

Two populations, four years (2016, 2017, 2018, 2019), training strictly on 2013 through validation year minus one. First Four excluded. The old reference and classifier code are byte-identical frozen copies of the completed experiments.

| Recipe | Inputs | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| anchor | 16 | Existing seeding/strength/efficiency/shooting reference |
| anchor_rates | 22 | Reference plus six ordinary offensive/allowed rates |
| anchor_mechanism | 20 | Reference plus four nonlinear candidates |
| anchor_both | 26 | Both additions |

Logistic C=0.1, no intercept, mirrored orientations, total physical-game weight 1, train-only RMS scaling, max_iter=2000, tol=1e-7, two computational threads. No hyperparameter optimization, ensemble, calibration sweep, or capacity-limited feature selection. Only training-constant columns can be removed.

**Primary comparison: anchor_both − anchor_rates**, separately by population and year. This asks whether the new representation adds information beyond direct rate inputs. Secondary contrasts add each family alone and remove it from the combined representation while holding all remaining features fixed.

The seven existing baseline classifiers are replayed against exact saved prediction keys, labels, probabilities and reported scores before training challengers. Two baseline model files were not externally hashed in the uploaded public reports: those use their complete-checkpoint hashes plus independently reported score replay, and their observed byte identities are recorded. Other externally available pins are checked as well. No pickle deserialization occurs.

Compute: 14 cached base snapshots; 14 cheap rate aggregations; zero rating fits; seven baseline replays; one new baseline plus 24 new challengers = **at most 25 new classifiers**, 32 comparisons total. Every new snapshot and classifier has a checksum-verified completion receipt. Incomplete work is not marked complete; corrupted completed artifacts stop rather than being silently replaced.

## 7. Decision, uncertainty and next-stage restrictions

All four seasons are examined together. A primary mean delta ≤ −0.0005, at least 3 improved seasons, and worst delta ≤ +0.003 earns consideration of unchanged later-era replication. It triggers no automatic compute. The basic-rate-only comparison is reported even if the nonlinear candidates fail. Existing concepts helping an underspecified reference cannot be called a new production improvement.

Repeated feature decisions on the same historical tournaments create selection bias; a fixed threshold does not undo it. Cawley and Talbot discuss overfitting model-selection criteria and resulting evaluation bias [5]. These years are openly labeled reused exploratory history. Small season counts, overlapping training sets and tournament-game dependence preclude an independent-test or significance claim.

The final submitted pooled XGBoost/conference-logistic recipe is not reproduced here. A favorable result requires later-era stability, controlled component tests where justified, and fixed-production-recipe transfer before submission claims. No 2026 labels may influence a 2026 forecast. Even a retrospectively better 2026 result would not establish a prospective leaderboard record.

## 8. Remaining high-value research, not executed here

The next choice depends on measured evidence. Major remaining investigations include rating-source/ordinal-scale complementarity with legal timestamp coverage; opponent-adjusted changes with calibrated uncertainty rather than noisy window differences; historically verifiable player availability, roster continuity and minutes; and representation transfer into the existing stronger final recipe. Published features and negative experiments must be inventoried before repeating them. A failed fixed recipe pauses that implementation, not an entire scientific topic.
